      What pressure is

      A ball is under pressure when the result hinges on it. For each delivery a win-probability model gives the expected swing across the six things that can happen next. The top of balls by that swing are pressure balls.

      Pressure peaks when eight to twelve an over is needed late in a chase, and fades both when the chase is easy and when it is already lost. Nobody told the model that; it falls out of the win probabilities.

      06

      Method

      The short version. The methodology report has the full account, the evidence tables and the limitations.

      1. Rebuild the match. Score, wickets and balls left before every delivery, checked against official margins in decided matches. Rain-revised matches and super overs are left out.
      2. Price the moment. Win probability from the match state, validated on held-out matches (chase AUC ). Pressure is the expected swing on the ball about to be bowled.
      3. Score the ball. Runs above what an average player manages in that situation against that opposition, with a dismissal priced by the batting resource it costs. Bowlers are scored the same way in reverse.
      4. Rate the player. Pressure record and usual level are blended by how informative each is and shrunk towards average for small samples. To be listed, a player's own pressure record must be above average and must not credibly dip below their usual level.
